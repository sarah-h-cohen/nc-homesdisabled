
I had Claude code create the sentinel_defs file by doing the following: 

Prompt: 

 have a list of 384 NCHS / ICD10 codes in the data frame called `death_codes`.  The context for these death codes is that they appeared somewhere in the record axis code field of a death certificate database that contained records on people who lived in intermediate care facilities for intellectual disabilities. I am looking at these codes to look for red flags that may suggeest some form of abuse, neglect, mistreatment or shoddy care.

Create a new data frame called sentinel_lookup that contains the following columns: 

1. code - the original code as shown
2. description - the original description as shown. 
3. plain_language_definition: A clear, concise explanation of the condition in plain English suitable for a non-medical reader. (e.g., for 'Dehydration', use 'Severe fluid loss often caused by lack of access to water or improper monitoring of intake').
4. care_focus_area: A categorical tag identifying the specific aspect of care (e.g., 'Skin Care', 'Respiratory', 'Mobility', 'General Medical', 'Neurological').
sentinel_level: 0 (Baseline), 1 (Expected), 2 (Vigilance), or 3 (High-Alert/Red Flag).

Ensure that the plain_language_definition remains specific to the diagnosis while removing medical jargon. 

Here is the goal: 

plain_language_defiinition: The "what", which turns the cod code into human-readable phrase or sentence. Example: Instead of just "Decubitus ulcer, sacral region, stage II," the column says: "Skin breakdown (bedsore) on the tailbone area caused by prolonged pressure and lack of repositioning.

care_focus_area: The "where: - group these codes by the type of service or department that should have been monitoring them. Example: Categories like "Hydration/Nutrition," "Skin Care," "Respiratory Support," "Mobility/Fall Prevention," or "Seizure Management."

sentinel_level: the"why" - classify the preventability or the nature of the condition.

Example: The 0–3 scale we discussed (0: Background, 1: Expected, 2: Vigilance, 3: High-Alert

How it's built

sentinel_defs is a table of code, plain-language definition, care area and level that you can edit directly.
sentinel_lookup joins death_codes to it, so code and description come straight from your sheet, unchanged.

The care areas are a fixed set of 21, including Swallowing/Aspiration, Hydration/Nutrition, Skin Care/Wound, Seizure Management, Mobility/Fall Prevention, Injury/Supervision and Infection Control.

Level 3 (red flags):

Choking and aspiration: aspiration pneumonia (J690, the most common code, 77 appearances), objects or food in the airway (T179, W79, W80, T178), inhaling stomach contents (W78).
Feeding and fluids: malnutrition (E43, E46), dehydration (E86), too much salt in the blood (E870).
Skin: bedsores (L893, L899).
Bowels: constipation and a severely stretched colon (K590, K593).
Falls and injuries: falls, fractures, head and brain injuries, injuries from an unrecorded cause, effects of an earlier assault.
Safety: suffocation (including in bed), drowning, cold exposure, poisonings and overdoses.
Calls you may want to revisit

Bowel blockage (K566, 21 appearances) is at 2, not 3. In ICF populations it is often caused by impacted stool, but blockages also have other causes. Only constipation itself is at 3.
Some codes describe how someone died, not why. Cardiac arrest (I469) is at 1. Respiratory failure and low oxygen or suffocation (J960, J969, R090) are at 2, and the R090 definition says to check whether choking was involved.
Bleeding in the skull recorded as "not caused by injury" (I620, I629) is at 2. The definition says to check it against fall and injury history.
F819 (a learning-skills disorder) appears 34 times and seems to be used here to note the developmental disability, so I put it at 0 and said so in its definition.
Weight loss, wasting and loss of appetite (R634, R64, R630) are at 2, not 3, because they're common at the end of cancer.
